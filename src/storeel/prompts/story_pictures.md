THE PICTURES (descriptions in English, for an image model; every picture is generated):
- Each beat shows up to two characters standing at the bottom of the frame (ids), and AT MOST ONE of: a prop, a photo, a chat, a note; plus optionally a tag.
  - prop: a scene, an object, a detail or an empty place.
    - A SCENE is the moment itself: its people doing what the words say, in its place ("Bu Tini hands Pak Darto an envelope at the end of the alley at night", with Laras, who watches it, standing at the bottom). Set "refs" to the characters in it (at most two; their portraits are used to draw them), "people" to the exact number of figures, and describe the place, the poses and the action fully: the people caught in the middle of what they do, never posed side by side facing the viewer. Describe it as a medium shot, the people large enough that their faces and feelings read on a phone. A scene is drawn wide and shown as a framed picture in the upper part of the screen, above anyone standing at the bottom. When something happens between people somewhere, show it as a scene of them doing it, not as an empty place with the people standing in front of it.
    - An OBJECT ("two identical red denture cases") is shown cut out of its background over the video; set "subject" to a short English noun phrase naming it for a segmentation model ("red denture case"), and "shape" to its outline ("tall" for a bottle, "wide" for a sword, else "square").
    - A DETAIL shot (hands gripping, an object held) gets "framing": "close-up".
    - An EMPTY PLACE (nobody in it: a dark kitchen, a street at night) gets "backdrop": true and is drawn wide, as a framed picture. Use it only to open a new setting before anything happens there, or for a mood with nobody in it; never as the background of people talking or acting there (that is a scene).
    - Describe what is there concretely: materials, colours, light, and what must NOT appear when it matters. Never show a character standing at the bottom AND inside a scene prop of the same beat.
  - note: writing the viewer must read - a note on the fridge, a sign, a text on a screen: {"text": "the words, at most 120 characters", "style": "paper|sign|screen"}. The image model cannot write, so NEVER ask for readable words, letters or numbers in a prop or photo: draw the object without writing and put the words in a note.
  - photo: a real-looking place, shown as a photo card with a caption ("title").
  - chat: one or two phone messages shown as chat bubbles: {"sender": "Ucup", "text": "..."} (at most 90 characters each).
  - tag: a red banner of one or two words for a reveal ("PLOT TWIST", "<<PREVIOUSLY>>").
- DIRECT IT LIKE A FILM. Plan each beat's screen the way a director plans a shot:
  1. SEE. Before choosing "show", write "see": one English sentence naming exactly who and what the viewer should be looking at while hearing this beat, and who is the focus. Then make "show" deliver exactly that.
  2. ONE FOCUS. Every screen has one focus: whoever or whatever the words are about at that moment, and it is the biggest thing on screen. The biggest thing is a character standing at the bottom; the card above shows what they see, hear, meet or remember.
  3. SOMEONE STANDS. Build almost every beat on a character standing at the bottom. Who stands:
     - While a character's words are quoted: that character (viewers take the standing figure for the one speaking, and their name is shown over them), with the one they talk to standing beside them or close on that one's face in the card above. Only for an act between the two of them (a whisper in the ear, a slap, an embrace) may the card be a scene of the two doing it, with nobody standing.
     - Otherwise: the one the beat is about, or the one who watches and feels what the scene above shows (the daughter who sees her father hide the letters).
     - With a scene of people up, at most one person stands: never more than three named people on screen at once.
     - Nobody standing is the exception, at most one beat in four: an empty place opening a new setting, an act that needs the people in it together, or a close-up of the face that carries the part's peak (it may be the speaker's own face during their line).
  4. THE CARD SHOWS WHAT HAPPENS. The card above shows the action of the words at that moment: who does what to whom, and where ("her father saw the stranger hand her the letters": the stranger and the daughter, the letters between them; "her neighbour told her to burn them": the neighbour pressing her). Never a person alone in a place, full figure: show them with the ones they act with, or close on their face as a reaction. An empty place only opens a setting before anything happens there, behind one person standing alone, never behind a conversation.
  5. THE STANDING FIGURES CANNOT ACT. They are the characters' fixed portraits, with a calm face. A feeling the viewer must see is shown in the card: close on the face, or a scene of the act. A character who stands through several beats in one strong state (in tears, on her knees, furious) gets a variant for it (see THE CHARACTER BIBLE), at most three per part.
  6. CUT ON THE WORDS. A new picture comes on the beat where its subject is first named or first acts: never before (it would give it away), never a beat later. Show only what has already been said or is being said.
  7. CLOSE ENOUGH TO READ. The card is small on a phone: a scene is a medium shot, the people filling most of its height with their faces and feelings clear and the place around them, never small figures in a big space. Go closer for the beat that carries the feeling: the cruel line, the reveal, the tears. A close-up is a scene prop with "framing": "close-up" of the face or the hands that show it (refs: that character, people: 1). The emotional peaks of the part are shown on faces.
  8. ANOTHER TIME, ANOTHER PICTURE. A memory or a flashback is its own beat with its own picture, drawn as it was then (younger, other clothes, the old place): one beat of at least about 40 characters for the whole memory, even when it spans years. It comes on the words that go back in time and leaves when the words come back to the present.
  - The person who speaks, is quoted or acts in the sentence is on screen. The main character stands only in the beats about them or in which they watch what the card shows.
  - Every person who speaks or acts on screen is a character, however minor (the relative who gives an order, the servants who stare): give them a character entry so they are designed once and look the same everywhere.
  - A reveal inside a long beat (about 80 characters or more) may switch the picture with "then": {"at": "two to five consecutive words copied exactly from the middle of the beat's text, where the switch happens", "show": {...}}. Use it only for a reveal, never just because the next words are about someone else. Without a switch, "then.at" is "".
- PACE. A picture needs time to be seen (at least three seconds), and a screen that changes every few seconds tires the viewer. Keep it calm:
  - One moment, one picture. The beats of one moment (one place, the same people, one exchange) keep the same picture: repeat its prop or photo id in each of those beats, with the same people standing. Change it when the story moves to another moment, place, time or person; inside a long moment, go closer once, for its peak.
  - A conversation is one moment: show both of them standing at the bottom for all its beats, with the card above on what they talk about or close on the face of the one the words hit. Never cut back and forth to whoever is speaking.
  - Never show the same picture again for a moment that has moved on, and never two scenes in a row of the same people doing the same thing.
- Almost every beat should show something. Use props for the objects the plot turns on.
- Characters are drawn as full-body portraits. "description" is who they are, the same in every chapter: apparent age, ethnicity, build, skin, face, hair, distinguishing marks - nothing they wear or hold. "outfit" is what they wear in THIS chapter: clothes with colours, shoes, headwear, accessories, chosen for where, when and why the chapter happens. Hands are empty unless they carry the thing through the whole chapter. "subject" is a short English noun for a segmentation model ("woman", "old man", "girl", "cat"). Injuries are shown tastefully, never gore.
- Never depict real public figures; describe a generic person instead.
- Write each picture's people so the image model draws them at the right age and its safety filter accepts the picture: give every adult an adult's age ("a woman in her early twenties", "a man in his fifties"; never "girl", "boy" or "young princess" for an adult), show feelings through the face and the pose (eyes wet with tears, a hand pressed to the chest), and leave out looks words (beautiful, handsome, sexy, seductive) and body words (lips, skin, body, waist). Children appear only as themselves in a memory, doing something ordinary.

THE CHARACTER BIBLE. The user message lists the characters already designed in earlier chapters: who they are ("description") and what they wore when last seen ("last_outfit").
- Reuse their ids. Never describe a bible character again in "characters".
- Dress them for THIS chapter. For every bible character the chapter shows (standing in a beat, or drawn in a scene prop's refs), add a "looks" entry: {"id": "<bible id>", "outfit": "English: what they wear in this chapter"}. Repeat the last_outfit only when this chapter continues the same day and nothing suggests they changed.
- If a character looks different only in some moments (an injury, a disguise, a ghost, soaked by rain, years younger in a memory, or a strong feeling held for several beats: in tears, on her knees, furious), add a "variants" entry: {"id": "<base>_<what>", "base": "<character id>", "change": "English: what is different", "persistent": true|false}. Show the variant id in the beats where it applies; the base id elsewhere.
- New characters (first seen in this chapter) go in "characters" with a new id (lowercase snake_case of their name), a short on-screen "label" (lowercase, as viewers would call them), "role", "description", "outfit", "subject" and "slot" ("left" or "right": where they usually stand).
- "bible_notes": short notes in <<LANGUAGE>> on lasting changes worth remembering for later chapters (a death, an injury, a secret revealed), one per character. Only facts the chapter states outright.
- A prop that will matter again later gets "recurring": true. A recurring prop is stored in the bible and reused as the same picture, so its description is the object ALONE: no place, table, hands or people.
- Bible props (listed in the user message) may be shown again by id only when the object itself, alone, is what the beat shows.
