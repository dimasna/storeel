THE PICTURES (descriptions in English, for an image model; every picture is generated):
- Each beat shows up to two characters standing at the bottom of the frame (ids), and AT MOST ONE of: a prop, a photo, a chat, a note; plus optionally a tag.
  - prop: an isolated object or a dramatic moment, e.g. "two identical red denture cases", or a scene with characters in it (set "refs" to their ids, at most two, and describe the action: "Agus kneels on the road cradling his grandmother").
    A scene prop draws its characters from their portraits, so describe the pose, place and action fully (lying, crouching, hiding behind a door, seen from behind...) and set "people" to the exact number of figures in it (a corpse or a ghost counts). It is cut out of its background: the figures and what they use (a bench, a bed, a tray) float over the video, so describe what they do and what they use, not the room around them. Each character appears once: never show a character standing at the bottom AND inside a scene prop of the same beat.
    A detail shot (hands gripping, a face, an object held) gets "framing": "close-up"; list in "refs" whose face or hands are in it, and describe the visible skin, sleeves, clothing (as they wear it in this chapter) and objects precisely ("an old woman's wrinkled hand with a thin gold ring gripping the navy blue apron of a young man"). Full scenes get "framing": "full".
    Describe what is there, concretely and consistently with the story and the setting: materials, colours, light, and what must NOT appear when it matters ("only the bare feet... no shoes", "dark red-brown soil, not blood").
    A prop that is a whole place or atmosphere (a dark kitchen, a street at night) rather than figures or objects gets "backdrop": true: it keeps its background and is shown as a framed picture. Figures and objects stay cut-outs (no backdrop).
    An object (no refs, no people, no backdrop) gets "subject": a short English noun phrase naming it for a segmentation model ("red denture case"). "shape" is its outline ("tall" for a bottle, "wide" for a sword or a scene, else "square"); "size" is how big it is shown ("small", "medium", "large").
  - note: writing the viewer must read - a note on the fridge, a sign, a text on a screen: {"text": "the words, at most 120 characters", "style": "paper|sign|screen"}. The image model cannot write, so NEVER ask for readable words, letters or numbers in a prop or photo: draw the object without writing and put the words in a note.
  - photo: a real-looking place, shown as a photo card with a caption ("title"), e.g. the clinic where the chapter starts.
  - chat: one or two phone messages shown as chat bubbles: {"sender": "Ucup", "text": "BET, ..."} (chat text may be caps, at most 90 characters).
  - tag: a red banner of one or two words for a reveal, e.g. "HILANG", "PLOT TWIST", "<<PREVIOUSLY>>".
- THE PICTURE FOLLOWS THE WORDS. While a sentence is spoken, the viewer must see who or what that sentence is about at that moment. Before choosing "show", write "see": one English sentence naming exactly who and what the viewer should be looking at while hearing this beat. Then make "show" deliver exactly that:
  - The person who speaks, is quoted or acts in the sentence is on screen. "Petugasnya bilang tenang" shows the clinic receptionist - not Agus, who only stands there. Never fill a beat with the main character by default.
  - Every person who speaks or acts on screen is a character, however minor: a receptionist, a paramedic, a doctor, a driver. Give them a character entry (id like "petugas_klinik", label like "petugas") so they are designed once and look the same everywhere.
  - When the subject changes inside a beat (one person speaks, then another answers), switch with "then" at the first words of the change, or split the beat.
  - Show only what has already been said or is being said; never a picture that reveals the next beat.
- MOST BEATS ARE THE CHARACTERS THEMSELVES, standing: the one who speaks or is quoted (alone, or with the one they talk to), two people facing each other, the one who reacts. Add a prop, photo, chat or note only when the words are about that object, place, message or writing, and a scene prop only for what standing portraits cannot show (someone on another's lap, an embrace, a fall, people at work together). A character in a strong state at that moment (in tears, pleading with clasped hands, frozen in shock, sick in bed clothes) or in a memory (years younger, other clothes) is shown standing as a variant of them (see THE CHARACTER BIBLE).
- Almost every beat should show something. Use props for the objects the plot turns on.
- TIMING. A beat's picture stays up while its text is spoken (about one second per fifteen characters). When a beat's text turns halfway ("...dan pas dia buka pintunya, ternyata..."), switch the picture on that word: add "then": {"at": "two to five consecutive words copied exactly from the beat's text, where the switch happens", "show": {same fields as show}}. Use it for reveals and for long beats, not for every beat. Without a switch, "then.at" is "".
- Characters are drawn as full-body portraits. "description" is who they are, the same in every chapter: apparent age, ethnicity, build, skin, face, hair, distinguishing marks - nothing they wear or hold. "outfit" is what they wear in THIS chapter: clothes with colours, shoes, headwear, accessories, chosen for where, when and why the chapter happens (at work, at home in the evening, a funeral or the prayer nights after a death, school, a wedding, rain). Hands are empty unless they carry the thing through the whole chapter (an old man's walking stick): an object that matters in one moment (a phone being checked, a letter, a tray of food) is a prop or a scene prop for that moment, otherwise the portrait holds it in every beat. "subject" is a short English noun for a segmentation model ("woman", "old man", "girl", "cat"). No background, no text. Keep to what the chapter says; invent the rest plausibly and consistently. Injuries are shown tastefully (bandage, bruise, sling), never gore.
- Never depict real public figures; describe a generic person instead.
- In scene and close-up descriptions, give every adult an adult's age ("a woman in her early twenties"; never "girl" or "young princess" for an adult) and leave out looks words (beautiful, handsome, sexy, seductive): the image model's safety filter refuses some of those pictures. Children appear only as themselves in a memory, doing something ordinary.

THE CHARACTER BIBLE. The user message lists the characters already designed in earlier chapters: who they are ("description") and what they wore when last seen ("last_outfit").
- Reuse their ids. Never describe a bible character again in "characters".
- Dress them for THIS chapter. For every bible character the chapter shows (standing in a beat, or drawn in a scene prop's refs), add a "looks" entry: {"id": "<bible id>", "outfit": "English: what they wear in this chapter"}, following the outfit rules above: what the chapter says they wear, otherwise what fits its place, time and occasion. Repeat the last_outfit only when this chapter continues the same day and nothing suggests they changed. Their portrait is redrawn from the bible in this outfit and is how they look in every beat of the chapter: same face, hair and body; new clothes; hands as the outfit says.
- If a character looks different only in some moments of the chapter (an injury, a disguise, a ghost, soaked by rain, in tears, pleading with clasped hands, years younger in a memory), add a "variants" entry: {"id": "<base>_<what>", "base": "<character id>", "change": "English: what is different from their look in this chapter", "persistent": true|false}. Describe the change fully, face and hands included ("tears running down her cheeks, eyes red, both hands clasped in front of her chest, no smile"). Show the variant id in the beats where it applies; the base id elsewhere. Other clothes for the whole chapter are a "looks" entry, not a variant.
- New characters (first seen in this chapter) go in "characters" with a new id (lowercase snake_case of their name), a short on-screen "label" (lowercase, as viewers would call them), "role", "description", "outfit", "subject" and "slot" ("left" or "right": where they usually stand).
- "bible_notes": short notes in <<LANGUAGE>> on lasting changes worth remembering for later chapters (a death, an injury, a secret revealed), one per character. Only facts the chapter states outright - never a guess about who holds or knows what - because later chapters are written from these notes.
- A prop that will matter again later gets "recurring": true. A recurring prop is stored in the bible and reused as the same picture, so its description is the object ALONE: no place, table, hands or people ("two identical red plastic denture cases", not "two denture cases on a clinic counter").
- Bible props (listed in the user message) may be shown again by id only when the object itself, alone, is what the beat shows. If the story needs it in a new place, state or scene, write a new prop (it may have refs) describing it there.

ANSWER with one JSON object with every field of the schema. Empty values: "" for a missing prop, photo, tag or then.at, [] for no chat, {"text": "", "style": "paper"} for no note.
